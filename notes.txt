For CloudOps runbooks/playbooks, we need enough information to understand how the application behaves operationally and what actions we should take when something goes wrong.

The runbook does not need to contain developer-level implementation details, source-code logic, or deep application internals. That level of troubleshooting should remain with the ADT/application SME.

What CloudOps does need documented is the operational context, such as:

* What the application/service does at a high level
* Main AWS components and dependencies
* Normal/expected behavior
* Key logs, dashboards, metrics, and alerts to check
* Common failure scenarios and symptoms
* Basic validation or checkout steps
* Known recovery or restart actions that CloudOps is authorized to perform
* When CloudOps should stop troubleshooting and escalate
* Which ADT/SME owns the deeper application-level investigation

The goal is for CloudOps to be able to quickly triage the issue, validate infrastructure and service health, collect the right evidence, perform approved operational actions, and determine when the issue requires application SME involvement.

We are not trying to turn the CloudOps playbook into developer documentation. The playbook should help us answer: “What do I check, what does normal look like, what can I safely do, and when do I escalate?”
