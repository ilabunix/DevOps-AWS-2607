## SLIDE 5 — HOW IT WORKS (4 min)

*Walk left to right through the pipeline slowly.*

"The architecture is entirely serverless, entirely AWS native.
No servers to patch, no agents to install, no data
leaving your environment.

Left to right — signals come in from your AWS accounts
via Amazon Kinesis. In the POC these are simulated.
In production these would be your CloudWatch log subscriptions."

*Point at anomaly detection.*

"Those signals hit our log processor Lambda, which runs
two detection algorithms.

First — a Bloom filter. This learns what normal looks like
for your logs. The moment it sees a pattern it has never
seen before — it fires. Novel patterns get flagged instantly.

Second — Z-score volume analysis. If your log volume
suddenly spikes or drops significantly, that's detected too.
A sudden drop in traffic is just as dangerous as a spike.

Those anomaly signals flow into the correlator.
The correlator groups related signals into incident clusters —
so instead of 50 alerts, you get one incident with 50 signals."

*Pause. Point at the AI Analysis step.*

"This is the important one. AI analysis via Amazon Bedrock.
Note what it says: on-demand, only when asked.

This is not automatic. Nothing calls Bedrock on a schedule.
Nothing calls Bedrock in the background. Bedrock is called
exactly once — when an engineer clicks a button or types
in the chat. That's how we keep costs predictable."

*Point at the purple banner.*

"Zero automatic calls. Zero surprise costs.
I want that to be very clear before I show you the live demo."

*--- ADD THIS BRIDGE — addresses objections before they're raised ---*

"Two questions I want to answer before we go live —
because they came up last time we showed this.

First — you may already have Grafana. That's a good thing,
not a conflict. Grafana and OpsIQ solve different problems.
Grafana shows you what the metrics are doing.
OpsIQ tells you what to do about it.
They run side by side — OpsIQ doesn't replace
anything you've already built.

Second — what gets deployed in your child accounts,
and is it safe from an InfoSec perspective?
The answer is three resources: one IAM role,
one subscription filter, one Kinesis Firehose.
The role has five permissions — three read-only on your
own logs, two write to one specific Kinesis stream.
Data flows one direction — out of child accounts
into the central hub. The central account never
reaches back in. I'll cover this in more detail
in the Q&A but I want you to know it was designed
with that constraint in mind from day one."

## HANDLING QUESTIONS — UPDATED

---

### "We already have Grafana — why do we need this?"

This is the most important objection. Answer it in two parts.

**Part 1 — Acknowledge, don't defend:**
> "That's a fair challenge and I want to give you a real answer.
> If you've got centralised dashboards in Grafana and alerting
> in place — you've solved the visibility problem. You can see
> everything. That's the right foundation."

**Part 2 — Close the gap:**
> "But here's the question I'd ask. When an alert fires at 2am
> and your on-call engineer opens that Grafana dashboard —
> what happens next?
>
> They see a spike. They know which service. Then they open
> CloudWatch, start querying logs, search Confluence for the
> runbook, ping someone on Slack who's seen this before.
> That's 30, 40, sometimes 60 minutes before they have a
> theory about what's actually wrong.
>
> Grafana answers 'what is the metric doing.'
> OpsIQ answers 'why is it doing that, what else is affected,
> and what do I run right now to fix it.'
>
> Grafana is the CCTV camera.
> OpsIQ is the analyst watching the footage who taps you
> on the shoulder and says — camera 4, east corridor,
> here's what happened, here's what to do."

**If they push further:**
> "The fact that you've already centralised in Grafana
> actually makes OpsIQ easier to deploy, not harder.
> Grafana reads your CloudWatch data. OpsIQ taps the same
> CloudWatch logs in parallel via a subscription filter.
> Your Grafana doesn't change. Your alerting doesn't change.
> OpsIQ is the layer above that turns your alerts into answers."

**The number that closes it:**
> "How many production incidents did your team investigate
> last quarter? And what was the average time from alert
> to understanding what was wrong?
>
> If that number is more than 15 minutes, OpsIQ pays for
> itself in the first incident. It costs $50 a month to run."

---

### "InfoSec won't allow cross-account access"

**Answer — clarify the model first:**
> "That's a reasonable concern and I want to be precise
> about what cross-account access actually means here —
> because it's not what most InfoSec teams are worried about.
>
> We're not asking for read access into child accounts from
> the central account. It's the opposite. Child accounts
> push data out. The central account never reaches in.
>
> It's the same model AWS itself uses for Security Hub,
> GuardDuty aggregation, and CloudWatch cross-account
> observability. It's in the AWS Well-Architected Framework."

**What InfoSec actually reviews:**
> "The child account agent is three resources.
> An IAM role with five permissions —
> three read-only on its own logs,
> two write to one specific Kinesis stream
> in the central account. That's it.
>
> The role cannot read from any other child account.
> Cannot write to anything except that one stream.
> Cannot access S3, DynamoDB, EC2, IAM, or any other service.
> If the credential is compromised, the blast radius is
> a single Kinesis stream — nothing else."

**If they still say no — offer Option B:**
> "If raw log data cannot leave the child account,
> we have a second architecture where the Bloom filter
> and Z-score detection run locally inside the child account.
> Only the anomaly event crosses the boundary.
> That event is a structured metadata record —
> account ID, anomaly type, severity, timestamp.
> About 100 bytes. No log content. No customer data. No PII.
>
> It's the same model as what AWS Security Hub sends
> between accounts today."

---

### "How does OpsIQ know the topology of our applications?"

> "Great question — and it's one of the most important ones
> for making this useful in production rather than just a demo.
>
> In the POC, topology is seeded manually. In production,
> it's discovered automatically from sources you already have.
>
> If you're running AWS Config — which most organisations
> do for compliance — we query the Config Aggregator.
> It already records every resource, every relationship,
> every configuration change in every account.
> ALB to ECS, ECS to RDS, Lambda to DynamoDB —
> Config knows all of it. We just query it and render it.
>
> If you use Terraform, we read the state file from S3.
> The Terraform state is the most accurate topology
> source that exists — it's exactly what was deployed
> and it includes the explicit dependency graph.
>
> If you have consistent resource tagging — tag Application=payments
> on every resource that belongs to the payments service —
> we query the Resource Groups Tagging API and group
> resources by application automatically.
>
> In most organisations you'd use two of these together.
> Config for discovery, Terraform state for dependency edges.
> Topology stays current automatically — when someone adds
> a new resource, Config records it within minutes,
> the graph updates, and the dashboard reflects it."

**What it needs from child accounts:**
> "For AWS Config — you likely already have Config recorder
> running for compliance. All you need is to add the child
> account to the Config Aggregator authorisation.
> One line of configuration, no new resources.
>
> For Terraform state — one S3 bucket policy change
> allowing the OpsIQ agent role to read the state file.
> Read-only access to one file.
>
> Either way — no new infrastructure in the child account
> beyond what we've already discussed."

---

### "How is this different from CloudWatch?"

> "CloudWatch shows you metrics. OpsIQ shows you meaning.
> CloudWatch tells you CPU is at 94%.
> OpsIQ tells you why it's at 94%, what else is affected,
> and what commands to run to fix it."

---

### "Is the AI reliable enough for production?"

> "The AI is advisory — it suggests, humans decide.
> Every output shows the confidence level and the evidence
> it used. A LOW confidence analysis is still faster
> than starting from scratch.
> The result is cached — you review it once,
> you never pay for it twice."

---

### "What if Bedrock is unavailable?"

> "The entire dashboard continues working —
> health scores, incident tracking, anomaly feed, topology.
> The Generate Summary button shows an error.
> Everything else is unaffected.
> Bedrock is additive, not critical path."

---

### "How does it scale to 100+ accounts?"

> "Kinesis shards scale horizontally.
> DynamoDB is on-demand billing.
> Lambda scales to zero.
> The only change at 100 accounts is adding Kinesis shards —
> that's a config change, not a redesign."

---

### "Who owns the AI model?"

> "You do. It runs in your AWS account,
> via your Bedrock subscription, billed to your AWS bill.
> Anthropic never sees your infrastructure data.
> Everything stays inside your environment."

---

### "What's the data retention?"

> "Incidents: 7 days, auto-purged by DynamoDB TTL.
> Anomalies: 3 days.
> Health scores: 15 minutes rolling.
> AI summaries: permanent — cached forever once generated.
> You never pay for the same analysis twice."

---

### "Can we use Grafana as the data source instead of connecting to CloudWatch directly?"

> "Grafana is a visualisation layer — it queries CloudWatch
> on demand for rendering panels but doesn't store logs itself.
> Querying Grafana for log data is like asking someone
> to read you a book they're currently reading.
> You'd be better off going to CloudWatch directly.
>
> If you're loading logs into Redshift via Grafana's data pipeline,
> then Redshift is a viable historical data source —
> but you'd query Redshift directly, not through Grafana.
> And for real-time anomaly detection you still need
> a streaming source — Kinesis gives you sub-second latency,
> Redshift queries have a minimum of several seconds.
>
> The simplest path is a CloudWatch subscription filter
> feeding the central Kinesis stream directly.
> Grafana keeps doing exactly what it does today.
> OpsIQ runs alongside it, not through it."
