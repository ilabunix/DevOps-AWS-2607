I actually think this is a strong story. The biggest thing I’d change is how you tell it.

Right now the slides explain what OpsIQ is.

Leadership cares more about why it matters.

Instead of walking slide-by-slide, tell it as a story:

“Here’s the problem we have today… here’s why existing tools don’t solve it… here’s how OpsIQ changes the workflow… and finally let me show you.”

That approach is much more compelling.

Opening (30 seconds)

Good morning everyone.

Today I’d like to share a proof of concept I’ve been building called OpsIQ.

The goal isn’t to replace CloudWatch, Grafana, or our existing operational tools.

The goal is to add an operational intelligence layer above them that helps engineers understand what’s happening across our AWS estate much faster.

⸻

Slide 1 — The Problem

Don’t read the slide.

Talk to it.

Today our CloudOps team manages roughly 20 to 30 AWS production accounts.

Whenever an alert fires, the investigation usually starts by jumping between multiple consoles.

We look at CloudWatch metrics…

then CloudWatch Logs…

maybe CloudTrail…

Terraform…

runbooks…

and eventually we build a picture of what happened.

None of these tools are broken.

The challenge is that they don’t connect the information together for us.

Engineers spend a significant amount of time gathering information before they can even begin solving the problem.

Pause.

That’s the problem I wanted to solve.

⸻

Slide 2 — Goals

The vision behind OpsIQ is fairly simple.

Instead of making engineers search for information…

bring the information to the engineer.

There are four goals.

Then explain each naturally.

Visibility

First, create a single operational view across AWS accounts.

⸻

Faster understanding

Second, reduce the time it takes to understand what’s happening.

Instead of manually correlating logs, metrics and events, have the platform assemble that context automatically.

⸻

AI Assistance

Third, use AI as an assistant—not as an autonomous operator.

The engineer remains in control.

AI simply summarizes information, identifies relationships, and recommends investigation steps.

⸻

Validate Architecture

Finally, this proof of concept validates that the end-to-end architecture works before connecting any production environments.

⸻

Slide 3 — Architecture

Don’t explain every AWS service.

Leadership doesn’t care.

Instead:

At a high level, the architecture follows five stages.

Then walk through them.

Signal ingestion

Operational signals arrive from CloudWatch logs and metrics.

⸻

Detection

The platform identifies unusual behavior and novel patterns.

⸻

Correlation

Instead of treating every alert independently,

related events are grouped together into a single incident.

⸻

AI

When requested,

Amazon Bedrock analyzes the correlated data and produces an operational summary along with possible root causes and investigation guidance.

Emphasize:

AI is invoked on demand.

It isn’t continuously generating responses or making operational decisions.

Leadership likes hearing this.

⸻

Dashboard

Finally everything is presented through a centralized dashboard where engineers can immediately understand overall health, incidents and anomalies.

⸻

Closing

This proof of concept demonstrates that an AI-assisted operational intelligence platform is technically feasible using AWS managed services.
